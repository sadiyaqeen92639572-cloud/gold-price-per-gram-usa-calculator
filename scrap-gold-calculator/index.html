Why a Mixed-Karat Lot Isn't Priced Like a Single Item

A single ring or chain is straightforward: one karat, one weight, one value. A scrap lot is different — many buyers weigh a mixed pile all at once and price the entire lot at its lowest karat unless you've sorted it first, since testing each piece individually takes them time. Sorting by karat before you sell (a cheap at-home magnet test plus a jeweler's acid test, or asking the buyer to test each piece separately) can meaningfully change what you're offered on a mixed lot. This calculator lets you enter each piece at its own real karat and weight so you can see what the lot is actually worth sorted, before you decide whether it's worth the extra step.

US Karat Standards

Karat quality marks (24K/22K/18K/14K/10K) follow the FTC's Jewelry Guides (16 CFR Part 23) — the federal rules on gold fineness marking. See the FTC's own consumer guide, Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry, for what to check before buying.
